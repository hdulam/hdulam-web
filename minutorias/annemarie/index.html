Section 1

Conic Sections

A conic section is what you get when you slice a cone at different angles. There are 4 types — and you need to recognize each one instantly.

🧠 The Big Picture: Every conic section comes from the same general equation: Ax² + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0. The values of A and C tell you what shape it is.
⬤ Circle
A = C (same, non-zero)
x² + y² = r²
∪ Parabola
Only x² OR only y²
y = a(x−h)² + k
⬭ Ellipse
A ≠ C, same sign
x²/a² + y²/b² = 1
⌇ Hyperbola
A and C opposite signs
x²/a² − y²/b² = 1

Core Skill

Completing the Square

This is THE most important skill for conics. Master this and you can convert any messy equation into a form you can read.

💡When do you use this? When you see something like x² + 6x + y² − 4y = 3 and need to find the center and radius. You "complete" each squared group into a perfect square.

📐 The Formula

x² + bx + (b/2)² = (x + b/2)²
The trick: Take the coefficient of x (call it b), divide by 2, square it → add to both sides. You just "completed the square."

Step-by-Step Method

1
Group x terms together and y terms together. Move the constant to the right side.
x² + 6x + ___ + y² − 4y + ___ = 3
2
For each group: take the middle coefficient, divide by 2, square it.
x group: 6÷2 = 3 → 3² = 9 | y group: −4÷2 = −2 → (−2)² = 4
3
Add those numbers inside the blank AND add them to the right side too (balance the equation).
x² + 6x + 9 + y² − 4y + 4 = 3 + 9 + 4
4
Factor each group into a perfect square trinomial.
(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 16
5
Read the answer! Center = (−3, 2), r = √16 = 4
Easy One Variable

Complete the square: x² + 8x

1
Take 8, divide by 2 → 4, square it → 16
2
x² + 8x + 16 = (x + 4)² ✅
Medium Full Circle

Convert: x² + y² − 10x + 4y − 7 = 0

1
(x²−10x + ___) + (y²+4y + ___) = 7
2
x: (−10/2)² = 25 | y: (4/2)² = 4
3
(x−5)² + (y+2)² = 7+25+4 = 36
4
Center = (5,−2), r = 6 ✅
⚠️ Common Mistake: Don't forget to add your completion numbers to the RIGHT side too! If you add 9 inside, you MUST add 9 to the other side or the equation is wrong.
🎯 Quick Tip: If the coefficient of x² or y² is NOT 1, factor it out first before completing the square.
Example: 2x² + 8x → factor: 2(x² + 4x) → complete inside: 2(x+2)² − 8
Section 2

⬤ Circles

A circle is all points equidistant from a center. The distance from center to edge is the radius.

Standard Form
(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²
  • • (h, k) = center
  • • r = radius
  • • Watch signs: (x−3)² means center x = +3
  • • (x+3)² = (x−(−3))² means center x = −3
General Form
x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0
To use this: Complete the square to convert to standard form. Then read center and radius.

Distance Formula (Essential!)

d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
💡 Use this to find the radius when given the center and a point on the circle, or the distance between any two points.
Easy Read Standard Form

Given: (x − 4)² + (y + 1)² = 49
Find center and radius.

1
h = 4, k = −1 → Center = (4, −1)
2
r² = 49 → r = 7
Medium From General Form

Given: x² + y² − 6x + 8y − 11 = 0

1
Rearrange: (x²−6x) + (y²+8y) = 11
2
Complete: +9 for x, +16 for y
3
(x−3)² + (y+4)² = 11+9+16 = 36
4
Center = (3, −4), r = 6 ✅
Exam Level Write Equation from Center + Point
A circle has center (2, −3) and passes through the point (5, 1). Write the standard form equation.
1
Find radius using distance formula: r = √[(5−2)² + (1−(−3))²] = √[9 + 16] = √25 = 5
2
Write equation: (x−2)² + (y+3)² = 25 ✅
Section 3

∪ Parabola

A parabola is the set of all points equidistant from the focus and the directrix. The vertex is the midpoint between them.

Vertex Form
y = a(x − h)² + k
  • • Vertex = (h, k)
  • • a > 0 → opens up ↑
  • • a < 0 → opens down ↓
  • • |a| large → narrow | |a| small → wide
Standard Forms with Focus & Directrix
(x−h)² = 4p(y−k) ← vertical (up/down)
(y−k)² = 4p(x−h) ← horizontal (left/right)
p = distance from vertex to focus (and vertex to directrix)
🎯 Focus, Vertex, Directrix Rules

Vertical Parabola (x² term)

  • • Vertex: (h, k)
  • • Focus: (h, k+p)
  • • Directrix: y = k − p
  • • p > 0 → opens up | p < 0 → opens down

Horizontal Parabola (y² term)

  • • Vertex: (h, k)
  • • Focus: (h+p, k)
  • • Directrix: x = h − p
  • • p > 0 → opens right | p < 0 → opens left
Key Relationship:
Every point on a parabola is the same distance from the focus as it is from the directrix.

Axis of Symmetry passes through vertex and focus.
Easy Read Vertex Form

y = 2(x − 3)² + 5

1
Vertex: (3, 5)
2
a = 2 > 0 → opens up
3
Narrow (|a|=2 > 1)
Medium Find Focus & Directrix

(x − 2)² = 12(y + 1)

1
Vertex: (2, −1). 4p = 12 → p = 3
2
Focus: (2, −1+3) = (2, 2)
3
Directrix: y = −1−3 = y = −4
Exam Level Write Equation from Vertex & Focus
Write the equation of a parabola with vertex (1, 3) and focus (1, 5).
1
Find p: Focus is directly above vertex → vertical parabola. p = 5 − 3 = 2
2
4p = 8. Opens up (p > 0).
3
Equation: (x − 1)² = 8(y − 3) ✅
⚠️ Common Mistakes:
• Confusing which variable is squared (x² = vertical parabola | y² = horizontal parabola)
• Forgetting that p can be negative (changes direction of opening)
• Mixing up focus and directrix directions
Section 4

⬭ Ellipse

An ellipse is the set of all points where the sum of distances to two foci is constant. Think of it as a "stretched circle."

Horizontal Ellipse (wider)
x²/a² + y²/b² = 1
(a > b, a² under x²)
  • • Center: (0,0) or (h,k)
  • • Major axis: horizontal, length = 2a
  • • Minor axis: vertical, length = 2b
  • • Foci: (±c, 0) where c² = a² − b²
Vertical Ellipse (taller)
x²/b² + y²/a² = 1
(a > b, a² under y²)
  • • Center: (0,0) or (h,k)
  • • Major axis: vertical, length = 2a
  • • Minor axis: horizontal, length = 2b
  • • Foci: (0, ±c) where c² = a² − b²
🔑 The Golden Rule: c² = a² − b²  |  a is ALWAYS the biggest denominator. The foci are always on the major axis. c < a always.
With center (h,k):   (x−h)²/a² + (y−k)²/b² = 1
Easy Read an Ellipse

x²/25 + y²/9 = 1

1
a² = 25 → a = 5, b² = 9 → b = 3
2
a² under x² → horizontal ellipse
3
c² = 25−9 = 16 → c = 4
4
Foci: (±4, 0) | Major axis: 2(5)=10
Exam Level Write from Axes

Ellipse with center (1,2), major axis = 8 (vertical), minor axis = 4.

1
a = 8/2 = 4, b = 4/2 = 2
2
Vertical → a² under y²
3
(x−1)²/4 + (y−2)²/16 = 1 ✅
4
c² = 16−4 = 12 → c = 2√3, Foci: (1, 2±2√3)
⚠️ Common Mistakes:
• a² is ALWAYS the LARGEST denominator — don't just assume it's under x²
• The equation must equal 1 (not 4 or 25) — divide if needed
• c² = a² − b² (not a² + b²)
Key Skill

🔍 Identifying Conics Fast

Given Ax² + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0, you can identify any conic in 3 seconds using the rules below.

Look at the coefficients A and C (the ones in front of x² and y²)
⬤
Circle
A = C

Same number, same sign in front of both x² and y²

⬭
Ellipse
A·C > 0, A≠C

Both x² and y² present, same sign, different numbers

∪
Parabola
A=0 or C=0

Only ONE of x² or y² is present

⌇
Hyperbola
A·C < 0

One positive, one negative (opposite signs)

⚡ Practice — Identify These Instantly

CIRCLE — A = 1, C = 1. Both equal ✅. Complete the square to find center and radius.
ELLIPSE — A = 4, C = 9. Both positive, not equal. Different values = ellipse ✅
PARABOLA — Only x² is present, no y² term. A=1, C=0 ✅
HYPERBOLA — A=1, C=−1. Opposite signs! A·C = −1 < 0 ✅
CIRCLE — A = 3, C = 3. Same value! Factor out 3 first, then complete the square ✅
Section 5

📝 Word Problems

This is where exams test you. Read carefully, draw a sketch, and set up coordinates. The math is always simpler than it looks.

Exam Level 🔭 Satellite Dish / Reflector Problem
A satellite dish is parabolic with the opening facing up. The dish is 12 feet wide and 3 feet deep. Where should the receiver be placed? (Find the focus.)
1
Set up coordinates: Place vertex at origin (0,0). Dish opens up → equation (x²) = 4p·y
2
Use a point on the rim: Width = 12, so edge is at x = 6. Depth = 3, so that point is (6, 3).
3
Substitute (6, 3): 6² = 4p(3) → 36 = 12p → p = 3
4
Focus at (0, p) = (0, 3) → The receiver should be placed 3 feet above the vertex ✅
Exam Level 🌉 Tunnel / Bridge Problem
A parabolic tunnel is 20 m wide and 8 m high at its center. A truck 5 m wide and 6 m tall wants to pass through. Will the truck fit? How much clearance is there at the truck's edge?
1
Set up: Center of tunnel at (0, 8). Opens down. Width = 20, so edges at x = ±10, y = 0.
2
Vertex form: y = a(x − 0)² + 8 = ax² + 8
3
Use point (10, 0): 0 = a(100) + 8 → a = −8/100 = −0.08
4
Truck edge at x = 2.5 (half of 5m wide): y = −0.08(2.5)² + 8 = −0.5 + 8 = 7.5 m
5
Truck height = 6 m < 7.5 m → Yes, it fits! Clearance = 7.5 − 6 = 1.5 m ✅
Exam Level 🏟️ Whispering Gallery (Ellipse)
A whispering gallery is elliptical. The room is 100 ft long and 40 ft wide. How far from the center are the foci? (This is where people must stand to whisper.)
1
Length = 100 → a = 50 | Width = 40 → b = 20
2
c² = a² − b² = 2500 − 400 = 2100
3
c = √2100 ≈ 45.8 ft from center (each focus)
4
People must stand ≈45.8 ft from center along the major axis ✅
Exam Level ⬤ Circle from 3 Points
Find the equation of the circle passing through (1,0), (0,1), and (−1,0).
1
General form: x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0. Substitute each point to get a system of equations.
2
Point (1,0): 1 + 0 + D + 0 + F = 0 → D + F = −1
3
Point (0,1): 0 + 1 + 0 + E + F = 0 → E + F = −1
4
Point (−1,0): 1 + 0 − D + 0 + F = 0 → −D + F = −1
5
From D+F=−1 and −D+F=−1 → D=0, F=−1, E=0
6
Equation: x² + y² − 1 = 0 → x² + y² = 1 (unit circle, center (0,0), r=1) ✅
Exam Level 🏈 Parabola from Vertex + Landing
A ball is kicked from the ground (0,0), reaches a max height of 20 ft at x=40, and lands at x=80. Write the equation.
1
Vertex = (40, 20) → y = a(x−40)² + 20
2
Use landing point (80, 0): 0 = a(80−40)² + 20 = 1600a + 20
3
1600a = −20 → a = −1/80
4
Equation: y = −(1/80)(x−40)² + 20 ✅

⋂ Set Theory

Sets are just collections of elements. The operations — union, intersection, complement — describe how sets combine or relate.

A ∪ B
Union

Everything in A OR B (or both)

A = {1,2,3} B = {3,4,5}
A∪B = {1,2,3,4,5}
A ∩ B
Intersection

Only what's in A AND B

A = {1,2,3} B = {3,4,5}
A∩B = {3}
A'
Complement

Everything in universe NOT in A

U={1..5} A={1,2,3}
A'={4,5}

Complex Expressions

Evaluate step by step — always do what's inside parentheses first.

Example A ∪ (B ∩ C)
1
A = {1,2,3}, B = {2,3,4}, C = {3,4,5}
2
First do B ∩ C: elements in both B and C = {3,4}
3
Then A ∪ {3,4}: everything in A or {3,4} = {1,2,3,4} ✅
Exam Level 👩‍🎓 Student Groups Problem
In a class of 30: 18 play soccer, 12 play basketball, 6 play both. How many play neither? How many play only soccer?
1
Total in S∪B = 18 + 12 − 6 = 24 (subtract those counted twice)
2
Neither = 30 − 24 = 6 students
3
Only soccer = 18 − 6 (both) = 12 students
📐 Inclusion-Exclusion Formula:
|A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|
This prevents double-counting the overlap.
🎯 How to recognize this: Any problem mentioning "both", "either", "neither", "or" with groups/sets → Draw a Venn diagram! Fill in the overlap (both) first, then work outward.
Section 7

🎲 Probability

Probability is the chance of an event occurring. Values always range from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).

P(event) = (favorable outcomes) / (total outcomes)
Key Rules
∪
Union (OR): P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)
∩
Intersection (AND): P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B|A)
′
Complement: P(A') = 1 − P(A)
Independent vs Dependent
Independent: One event doesn't affect the other.
P(A and B) = P(A) · P(B)
Dependent: First event changes the pool.
P(A then B) = P(A) · P(B|A)

Conditional Probability

P(B|A) = P(A ∩ B) / P(A)    "Probability of B GIVEN A already happened"
Think of it as: You've shrunk the sample space. Given A happened, what fraction of those cases have B too?

🃏 Card Problems (Standard Deck: 52 cards)

Know these:
• 52 total cards
• 4 suits: ♠ ♥ ♦ ♣ (13 each)
• 13 ranks per suit (A,2–10,J,Q,K)
• 26 red (♥♦), 26 black (♠♣)
• 4 of each rank (e.g., 4 aces)
Example Without Replacement

P(ace then king), no replacement

1
P(ace) = 4/52
2
P(king | ace drawn) = 4/51 (51 left)
3
P(ace then king) = (4/52)·(4/51) = 16/2652 ≈ 0.006

Always find totals for rows and columns first!

🧮 Bayes' Theorem

P(A|B) = [P(B|A) · P(A)] / P(B)
Exam Level Medical Test
1% of people have a disease. A test is 90% accurate (true positive). If someone tests positive, what's the probability they actually have the disease?
1
P(Disease) = 0.01, P(No Disease) = 0.99
2
P(+ | Disease) = 0.90 | P(+ | No Disease) = 0.10 (false positive)
3
P(+) = (0.90)(0.01) + (0.10)(0.99) = 0.009 + 0.099 = 0.108
4
P(Disease|+) = (0.90 × 0.01) / 0.108 = 0.009/0.108 ≈ 8.3% ✅

🔍 Checking Independence

Two events A and B are independent IF:
P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)

Check: calculate both sides. If equal → independent. If not → dependent.
Section 8

💡 Conceptual Questions

Exams often ask you to explain concepts, not just calculate. Here's how to answer these perfectly.

Independent: One event happening does NOT change the probability of the other.

Example: Flipping a coin and rolling a die. Getting heads doesn't affect what you roll.

P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B)
Mutually Exclusive: They CANNOT happen at the same time. P(A∩B) = 0.

Example: Rolling a 3 OR rolling a 5 on one die — can't get both at once.

P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B) (no overlap to subtract)
⚠️ Trick: Mutually exclusive events are NEVER independent (unless one has P=0). If they can't happen together, knowing one happened tells you the other definitely didn't!
P(B|A) = "The probability of B, given that A already happened."

When A happens, we shrink our sample space to only the cases where A occurred. Then we ask: within those cases, how many also have B?

Real-world example: P(it rains tomorrow | it's cloudy today) — you're not asking about all days, only cloudy ones.

Formula: P(B|A) = P(A∩B) / P(A)
The foci move closer together → toward the center.

As a → b (major and minor axes become equal), c = √(a²−b²) → 0. When c = 0, there's only one focus — the center — and the ellipse IS a circle.

A circle is just a perfect ellipse where both foci are the same point (the center).
The vertex is the midpoint between the focus and directrix.

• The focus is a point inside the parabola.
• The directrix is a line outside the parabola, on the opposite side from the focus.
• Every point on the parabola is equidistant from the focus and the directrix.
• The vertex is the point on the parabola closest to both the focus and directrix.
• Distance from vertex to focus = distance from vertex to directrix = |p|
In Ax² + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0:

• A = C (both same sign, non-zero): Circle — equal "stretch" in both directions
• A ≠ C (both same sign): Ellipse — different stretch, but still closed curve
• A = 0 or C = 0 (not both): Parabola — only one dimension is squared → open curve
• A and C have opposite signs: Hyperbola — the equation has a minus sign making two separate branches

Think of it as: same sign = closed shape, opposite sign = open/split shape, missing squared term = parabola.
Final Section

🛟 Exam Survival Guide

4 steps to solve ANY problem, plus the mindset that will carry you through tomorrow.

⚡ How to Solve ANY Problem in 4 Steps
1
IDENTIFY the type. What shape/topic is this? Look at the equation structure. Does it have x² and y²? One or two? Are they equal? Are the signs the same?
2
CONVERT to standard form. If it's messy (general form), complete the square. Get it to the clean form you memorized.
3
READ the answer. Once it's in standard form, just match to the formula. Center, radius, vertex, a, b, p — read it directly.
4
CALCULATE what's asked. Foci? Use c² = a² − b². Focus of parabola? Use p. Distance? Use the distance formula.

🌙 Last-Day Tips

01
Draw First

Sketch the shape before anything. A drawing tells you direction, where the focus goes, which axis is major.

02
Watch Signs

(x−3)² → center at +3. (y+5)² → center at −5. Signs flip when you move it to the other side.

03
Probability: List First

For "without replacement", reduce the denominator by 1 each time you draw.

04
Set Up Axes

For word problems, place the vertex or center at the origin when possible. Makes the math much easier.

05
Standard Form First

NEVER try to read a general form directly. Always complete the square first, then read.

06
Check Both Sides

When completing the square, always add the same number to BOTH sides of the equation.

07
Venn Diagrams

Always draw a Venn diagram for set theory problems. Fill in overlap first, then outer regions.

08
Don't Panic

If stuck, write what you know, identify the type, and try to get to standard form. Partial credit counts!

🏆 You've got this. Conics follow patterns. Probability follows rules. Once you recognize the type of problem, you always know exactly what to do. That's all math is — pattern recognition + a few formulas.
